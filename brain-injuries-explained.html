Brain injuries explained

Clear, honest information about acquired brain injury to help you understand what has happened and find a way forward.

Definition

What is an acquired brain injury?

An acquired brain injury is damage to the brain that happens after birth. It is not hereditary or caused by a degenerative condition. It changes how a person thinks, feels, and moves.

Traumatic injury This comes from a blow or jolt to the head. Falls, road accidents, and assaults are common causes.

Non-traumatic injury This comes from inside the body. A stroke, a lack of oxygen, a tumour, or an infection like meningitis.

Effects

Common effects of a brain injury

A brain injury can change many things at once. These effects are not a sign of weakness. They are a normal response to a complex injury.

Refer Get In Touch

Physical changes

Persistent fatigue that sleep does not fix. Headaches, dizziness, and trouble with balance or coordination.

Cognitive changes

Problems with memory, concentration, and planning. Processing information can take much longer than it used to.

Emotional changes

Sudden mood swings, anxiety, and a deep sense of loss. You might feel irritable or overwhelmed by things that once felt small.

Behavioural changes

Acting without thinking or struggling to start tasks. Social situations can feel exhausting and confusing.

Recovery

The journey of recovery after a brain injury

Recovery is not a single event. It is a long road with many stages. The path looks different for everyone but you do not have to walk it alone.

The injury and acute hospital care

The immediate focus is on saving life and stabilising the brain.

Inpatient rehabilitation and therapy

A team works with you to relearn skills and build strength.

Community reintegration and long-term support

The real work of building a good life back in the world begins.
